# A content engine without a marketing team.

You do not need to write all day to stay visible. You need one good idea a week and a system that turns it into everything else.

## Key takeaways

- Change the unit of work: one real idea a week, turned into an article, a clip, an email and a few posts.
- AI drafts and reformats; you supply the idea and the edit, which are the parts that matter.
- Write a one-page voice guide so the drafts sound like you, and always edit before publishing.
- Measure inquiries and replies, not likes, and review once a month.

Most small businesses know they should post. Most stop after three weeks. The reason is rarely a lack of ideas. It is that every piece of content starts from a blank page and takes an hour of someone who is also running the business.

The fix is to change the unit of work. Instead of writing five separate things, you produce one real idea each week and turn it into five formats.

## One idea, five formats

Take a question a customer asked this week, a job you finished, or an opinion you hold. Record two minutes on your phone explaining it. From that single source:

- **A short article** for your website, which also feeds search and AI answers.
- **A short video or clip** cut from the recording.
- **An email** to your list, with the idea in three sentences and one link.
- **Two or three social posts** with different angles.
- **An FAQ entry** if the question will come up again.

AI does the drafting and reformatting. You supply the idea and the judgment.

## The weekly rhythm

| Day | Task | Who | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Pick the idea and record two minutes | You | 10 min |
| Tuesday | Drafts of the article, email and posts | AI | automatic |
| Wednesday | Edit for accuracy and voice, approve | You | 30 min |
| Thursday | Schedule across channels | AI and a template | automatic |
| Friday | Check what worked, note next week's idea | You | 10 min |

The human parts are short and they are the parts that matter: the idea and the edit.

## Teach it your voice

Generic AI writing is the biggest risk. It sounds like everyone, so it persuades no one. Google's own guidance is to publish helpful, people-first content that shows real experience, however it is produced ([creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)). Fix the voice problem once, properly:

- Write a one-page voice guide: how you speak, words you use, words you never use, example sentences.
- Give the AI three or four things you have written that sound like you.
- Always edit the first draft. Cut anything that sounds like a brochure, add a real example, and keep the specifics.

## Plan from your pillars, not from the calendar

Pick three or four themes your customers care about, for example "what it costs and why", "how the job goes", "common mistakes" and "behind the work". Rotate through them. A pillar list removes the Monday question of what to post.

## Reuse what already works

Once a month, look back at the pieces that brought real inquiries and update them. A refreshed article that already ranks and answers a common question is worth more than a new post nobody asked for. Add the new details, remove what is out of date and republish with a visible update date.

## Measure what pays

Likes are pleasant and rarely matter. Track:

- Inquiries or calls that mention a piece of content
- Visits to your service pages from the articles
- Replies to the emails
- Whether you are named when people ask an AI assistant about your area

Review once a month and do more of what brings the right inquiries.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Publishing unedited AI text.** It reads as filler and puts off the readers you want.
- **Posting everywhere at once.** Start with your website, one social channel and email.
- **Making up facts or testimonials.** Only publish what is true and approved.
- **Stopping after a month.** Consistency over a quarter beats a burst of effort.

## A four-week start

Week one: voice guide and pillars. Week two: first idea and first full set. Weeks three and four: repeat, and fix what felt slow. By the end of the month you will have about four articles, four emails and a dozen posts, and a routine that fits in an hour or two a week.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a small business publish?

Once a week on your website and email is a realistic rhythm that you can keep up for a quarter. Consistency matters more than volume.

### Can I publish AI-written content?

Google says it rewards helpful, people-first content regardless of how it is produced. In practice that means a person should add real experience and check every fact before it goes out.

### Where should I publish first?

Start with your own website, one social channel where your customers are, and a short email list. Add more only when the routine is stable.
