Get named
in the AI answer.

More customers ask an assistant who to hire. A local business can improve its chances with a short list of unglamorous fixes.

Key takeaways

  • AI assistants name only a few local businesses, and ranking well in classic search does not guarantee a mention.
  • The signals are mostly basics: consistent business facts, recent reviews, clear service pages, answered questions and structured data.
  • Measure by testing real customer questions in several assistants each month and writing down who is named and why.
  • The SOCi figures describe large multi-location brands: use them as direction, not as a forecast for one business.

A customer used to search for a plumber, open three listings and call one. Now a growing share of them ask an assistant: "Who is a good plumber near me who does emergency calls?" The assistant answers with one to three names, and the rest of the market is invisible.

How big is the gap?

A 2026 local visibility index from the vendor SOCi, covering about 350,000 locations of 2,751 multi-location brands, reported that AI platforms recommended only a small share of locations: about 1.2% on ChatGPT and 7.4% on Perplexity, compared with about 36% appearing in Google's local pack (coverage by Search Engine Land). It is vendor data about large brands, so treat the exact numbers as direction rather than a forecast for one shop. The message is still useful: being found the old way does not guarantee being named in an AI answer.

The same research reported that the business details assistants repeat are often wrong or incomplete. That is good news for a business that keeps its facts clean, because most competitors do not.

What assistants lean on

Different systems use different sources, and they change over time. Across most of the guidance, the same signals keep appearing:

Your business factsWebsite, Google Business Profile, directoriesSame name, address, phone, hours and services everywhere
ReviewsGoogle, Yelp, industry sitesAsk for recent reviews and reply to them
Clear service pagesYour own websiteOne page per service and area, written in plain language
Questions and answersYour websiteAn FAQ that answers what customers actually ask

Third-party mentionsLocal press, associations, directoriesA few trusted listings beat many weak ones

A seven-step checklist

1. Fix the basics

Check that your name, address, phone and opening hours are identical on your website, your Google Business Profile (Google’s guide to improving local ranking) and the main directories. Small inconsistencies make assistants unsure which version to trust.

2. Write a page for every service and area

A page called "Services" is not enough. A page called "Emergency boiler repair in Austin" tells both people and machines exactly what you do and where.

3. Answer real questions

Collect the questions customers ask on the phone and write short, direct answers. Put them on the page they belong to, in full sentences. Assistants prefer text that answers a question in the first line.

4. Add structured data

Mark up your business, services, FAQs and reviews with schema.org data so machines do not have to guess. Google explains the basics in its introduction to structured data. One analysis of ChatGPT citations found most cited pages carried schema markup, though that figure came from a vendor and was not independently verified. It costs little and it is worth doing.

5. Collect and answer reviews

Recent, specific reviews are among the strongest signals. Ask every happy customer, make it a two-click step, and reply to each review in a sentence or two.

6. Earn a few trusted mentions

A local newspaper, a trade association or a respected directory is worth more than dozens of weak listings. Ask for a link where you can.

7. Let machines read the site

Keep your pages in plain HTML, load fast, and do not hide important text behind scripts or pop-ups. Make sure you are not blocking the crawlers you want.

How to measure it

There is no official ranking for AI answers, so measure by testing. Once a month, ask three or four assistants the questions a customer would ask, for example "best [your service] in [your city]" and note whether you are named, which sources are cited and what they say about you. Write the results down. The trend matters more than any single answer.

Be realistic

No one can guarantee a mention. What you can do is make your business easy to understand, easy to verify and well reviewed, which also helps your normal search results.

What to avoid

Do not stuff pages with keywords, publish dozens of thin city pages, or buy reviews. They are easy to spot, and they are the kind of signals both search engines and assistants learn to ignore.

Frequently asked questions

Can I pay to be recommended by ChatGPT?

No. Organic answers have no paid placement. You can make your business easier to find, verify and trust, which is what the checklist above does.

Do I need structured data?

It is not a guarantee, but it removes guesswork for machines and costs little. Mark up the business, services, FAQs and reviews with schema.org.

How long until I see results?

Fixes to business facts and profiles can show up within weeks. Reviews and third-party mentions build over months, so track the same questions every month.
